I. Statement of Facts
  1. On or about February 15, 2025, Eagle Transport LLC ("Carrier") was retained by Gulf Coast Freight Brokers, LLC ("Broker") to transport a load of packaged industrial goods (Load #GCF-2025-0891) from Memphis, Tennessee to a consignee in Gulfport, Mississippi, at an agreed all-in rate of $2,950.00.
  2. Carrier's driver, operating Unit #ET-14, picked up the load on time at 0545 hours on February 15, 2025. The shipment was inspected at origin and the Bill of Lading (BOL) was signed clean, with no noted exceptions. All cargo was sealed and undamaged at time of pickup.
  3. The shipment was delivered to the consignee on February 16, 2025 at 1210 hours, fourteen (14) hours ahead of the scheduled delivery window. The Proof of Delivery was signed by consignee representative James D. Okafor, who noted no damage and made no exception on the delivery receipt.
  4. Despite timely, complete, and damage-free delivery confirmed by signed documentation, Broker refused to remit payment, citing alleged "cargo damage" — a claim that is directly and unambiguously contradicted by the signed Proof of Delivery in Carrier's possession.
  5. On February 28, 2025, rather than attempting good-faith resolution of the payment dispute through lawful means, Broker — through Ms. Patricia A. Hendricks — submitted a FreightGuard report falsely stating that Carrier "caused cargo damage and abandoned communication." This characterization is fabricated in its entirety.
  6. As a direct and proximate result of this false report, Eagle Transport LLC has been flagged on the FreightGuard platform, causing multiple prospective brokers to refuse engagement, resulting in the immediate and continuing loss of commercial loads and revenue.
II. The FreightGuard Report — Verbatim Text

The report submitted by Ms. Patricia A. Hendricks on February 28, 2025 states, verbatim:

"Carrier caused cargo damage and abandoned communication after delivery. Driver was unresponsive for 72 hours. Bill of lading was altered. Do not use. Carrier is non-compliant and poses a significant business risk."

Each and every material statement in the foregoing report is demonstrably false. Carrier possesses the following documentary evidence directly refuting each claim: (1) the original, unaltered, consignee-signed Bill of Lading; (2) the Proof of Delivery signed by consignee James D. Okafor noting no damage; (3) GPS and ELD records showing continuous, uninterrupted transport; and (4) a complete email and telephone log demonstrating no lapse in communication.

III. Legal Authority
  1. Defamation / Commercial Libel (Mississippi Common Law): Under Mississippi law, a false statement of fact published to third parties that damages the business reputation of a plaintiff constitutes actionable defamation per se. The report at issue was published to all subscribing brokers on the FreightGuard platform — a commercial database with industry-wide reach — and has directly caused measurable economic harm. See Speed v. Scott, 787 So. 2d 626 (Miss. 2001).
  2. Tortious Interference with Business Relations: Mississippi recognizes a claim for tortious interference with prospective business advantage where a defendant, without legal justification, intentionally interferes with a plaintiff's business relationships through improper means. See Levens v. Campbell, 733 So. 2d 753 (Miss. 1999). Broker's submission of a knowingly false report constitutes precisely such interference.
  3. 49 U.S.C. § 14915 — False Statements: Federal law prohibits the making of false statements to federally regulated transportation databases and reporting systems. The submission of false information regarding a regulated motor carrier to a commercial carrier rating service is actionable under this statute.
  4. 49 CFR Part 386 — FMCSA Enforcement: FMCSA regulations prohibit parties from submitting materially false or misleading information regarding a motor carrier's safety, compliance, or business practices. Broker's false report implicates these federal regulatory provisions.
  5. Lanham Act § 43(a), 15 U.S.C. § 1125(a): False statements of fact in commerce that damage a competitor or business entity are actionable as false advertising/commercial disparagement under the Lanham Act, where, as here, the false statements were made in the context of commercial competition and interstate commerce.
